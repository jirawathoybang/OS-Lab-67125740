1. The Convoy Effect (Phase 2):

In the `cpu_scheduler.py` script, the Convoy Effect occurred as follows:
- FCFS executed Process P1 first because it arrived at the beginning of the ready queue.
- Process P1 required 10 CPU time units, causing P2 and P3 to wait even though they required only 2 and 3 units.
- P2 had to wait 10 time units, while P3 had to wait 12 time units.
- As a result, FCFS produced an average waiting time of 7.33 units and an average turnaround time of 12.33 units.
- Round Robin reduced the average waiting time to 4.33 units by allowing P2 and P3 to execute before P1 completed.

2. Choosing the Time Quantum:

In the Round Robin scheduling algorithm, the Time Quantum affects both fairness and system performance:
- Extremely Large Quantum (100): Each process can finish before its time slice expires. As a result, Round Robin behaves like FCFS and may cause the Convoy Effect.
- Extremely Small Quantum (0.0001): The CPU switches between processes very frequently, causing excessive Context Switching overhead.
- Performance Impact: Frequent context switching consumes CPU time for saving and restoring process states instead of executing useful tasks.
- Therefore, the OS must choose a suitable Time Quantum to balance responsiveness, fairness, and scheduling overhead.

Based on the results from `ai_inference_scheduler.py`:
